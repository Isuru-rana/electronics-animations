Electronics Animations

Interactive classroom animations for basic electronics. Runs in any browser, works offline.

Semiconductors and the PN Junction

Six scenes: conductor and insulator, pure silicon with heat, N and P doping, the depletion region, forward bias and reverse bias.

Diode Knee Voltage and How a Voltmeter Measures

5 V supply, 10 kΩ pot, 330 Ω and a 1N4007. Every point shows its voltage. Each meter reads V(red) minus V(COM): with no current both sides of the resistor sit at the same voltage, so it reads 0 V. Drag the probes, record points and plot the diode curve.

Zener Regulator

Vin 0 to 12 V, RS 330 Ω, 5.1 V Zener and a 1 kΩ or 470 Ω load. See the Zener take the spare current, when regulation stops, and the operating point move on the Zener reverse characteristic. Faults: Zener fitted backwards, and RS too small so the Zener overheats and burns.

Centre Tap Full Wave Rectifier

12-0-12 V transformer, two 1N4007 and a 1 kΩ load. Watch D1 and D2 take turns, the current through the load stay in one direction, and the output graph build up. Includes an open D2 fault.

Bridge Rectifier

12 V transformer, four 1N4007 and a 1 kΩ load. D1 and D2 conduct on one half, D3 and D4 on the other, with live input and output graphs. Includes an open D3 fault.

Transistor as a Water Tap

A water tap beside an NPN circuit (2N3904, 10 kΩ base, LED + 330 Ω). One slider turns the handle and drives the base: see cut-off, active (IC = β IB) and saturation on both sides.

Amplifier Stages

Mic, pre-amp, power amp and speaker. The power amp is made for about 1 V in, but a mic gives only 5 mV, so the speaker stays quiet even at full power amp gain. A pre-amp first widens the signal like a funnel until it fits the power amp. Live funnel view and scopes.

Amplifier Gain

A sine goes into an amplifier drawn as a funnel. Turn the gain knob: the output grows and the equation beside it fills in the measured voltages, Av = Vout ÷ Vin. Change Vin and the gain stays the same. Shows clipping at ±9 V.

ECG Amplifier

A heartbeat gives about 1 mV at the electrodes. One op-amp stage (×10) gives 10 mV, too small for the monitor. Add a second and third stage: gains multiply to ×1000, the monitor gets 1 V and shows the heart rate.

Summing Amplifier

LM358 inverting summing amplifier with R1 = R2 = 10 kΩ. Watch I1 and I2 meet at the 0 V node and flow on through Rf, with V1, V2 and Vout on three scopes: Vout = −(V1 + V2). Modes: two DC inputs, sine plus DC from a pot (Activity 8), and a two-sine audio mixer.

How an Op-Amp Works

Six scenes on a 741: open loop against closed loop (saturation and feedback), inverting and non inverting amplifiers with a live scope, gain control with a volume knob, noise cancelling headphones, and a differential amplifier removing hum from a long cable.
